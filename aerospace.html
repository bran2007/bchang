Aerospace

My experience with software, mechanical/electrical engineering, and project management.

From 2021 ~ 2026, I was a member of the COC Astronomy & Science Team, serving as Software Lead and Principal Scientist (research lead) for multiple NASA High Altitude Student Platform (HASP) missions. During that time, our team became the only community college selected to fly alongside major research universities, developing scientific payloads that operate in the upper stratosphere aboard NASA balloon flights. My work has focused on the intersection of embedded software, instrumentation, optics, and systems engineering. As one of the few high schoolers on the team, I learned plenty from older team members and advisors from Caltech + local astronomy groups.

SpectraSolis, pictured above represented our most ambitious optical payload to date. Working alongside subsystem leads, Caltech and UC Berkeley researchers, and local astronomers, we refined the optical alignment of the coronagraph, improved telemetry packet structures, and validated the spectroscopy pipeline through multiple design reviews and environmental tests. The project required balancing scientific objectives with strict mass, power, thermal, and flight reliability constraints imposed by the HASP team at Louisiana Space Consortium.

Below are a few images from July of 2026, when I got to travel to NASA site (CBSF) in Palestine, Texas, for the very first time for thermal-vac and integration. The payload will launch in early-September, floating above a stratospheric balloon for 12~20 hours up to ~122,000 feet.

Much of the software development occurred long before launch. I designed and maintained telemetry parsing software, automated health monitoring tools, and sensor validation pipelines to verify hundreds of incoming measurements during integration and testing. During thermal vacuum testing at NASA's Columbia Scientific Balloon Facility, these tools allowed us to quickly identify sensor anomalies, monitor system performance in real time, and verify that every subsystem continued operating under simulated stratospheric conditions. Throughout each mission, I also communicated monthly technical milestones to the NASA Balloon Program Office and the Louisiana Space Consortium while coordinating software development across multiple engineering subteams.
